# UI system

The first weather page uses Inter for numeric values and UI text, MiSans for Chinese glyphs, and Roboto Mono for English labels and metadata. These are semantic roles, not separate choices for individual weather scenarios.

| Role | Tracking | Numerals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labels | `--tracking-label: -.02em` | ordinary text |
| UI / descriptions | `--tracking-text: -.015em` | ordinary text |
| Values and units | `--tracking-number: -.02em` | tabular Inter |
| Large temperature | `--tracking-display: -.04em` | tabular Inter |
| Solar time | `--tracking-time: .01em` | tabular Inter |

Weather and control icons share a 32-unit canvas, flat line ends, lightly rounded joins and a bolder `--icon-stroke` (0.34% of the panel width, clamped to 2–3.4 CSS pixels). Geometry uses `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` so different display sizes do not accidentally create different line weights. The larger solar diagrams share the same stroke token and 172×100 viewBox. Sunrise retains the established straight radial fan; sunset uses three continuous concentric semicircles with radii 80, 54 and 28, without a horizon or ray fan. Both diagrams share the same outer geometric bounds (x=6–166, y=18–98), viewBox, CSS size and stroke. Neither diagram uses a directional arrow. Select the diagram from the upcoming solar event, independently of the illustrated weather scene. Weather forms are deliberately geometric: eight sun rays, one crescent, a single three-arc cloud, three rain strokes, two snowflakes, two wind lines and an open lightning zigzag. Avoid miniature decorative stars, layered rain/moon/thunder combinations and filled emblems.

The weather hero uses the same equal-width columns as the metric grid. Temperature and the solar diagram share a baseline track, range and solar label share the next row, and the weather description shares its text baseline with the event time. The weather symbol is centered beside the description but does not determine the text baseline. All support rows use `--hero-row-gap: 1.6cqw`; a hidden next-day caption removes its row entirely. The temperature is sized to stay inside its half of the grid, with a separate smaller tier for three-character readings. Its unit shares the number's baseline with a compact 0.025em gap. The cloud-cover reading is centered as one baseline-aligned value/percent group inside its track.

The gear's drawing canvas is 4.62cqw, with a minimum 44px hit area and no surrounding circle; keyboard focus uses a short underline. Its SVG box and the header time share the same right alignment line. City, air-quality text, date and time all inherit 2.65cqw Roboto Mono and share a text baseline. The air-quality dot is positioned independently of text metrics. Warning badges display the US AQI standard and value; the complete classification remains in the tooltip and accessible label so long descriptions cannot force a new header row. `--header-top-inset: 3.4cqw` moves the complete title/dots/gear row down while the metadata retains its bottom inset. Wider screens use a height-constrained hero to retain the next-day caption. Changes must be checked in Chinese and English, at the target tablet proportion and the affected viewport.

Fine artwork follows the native canvas dimensions, capped at 1536 columns and the GPU's maximum texture size; block artwork retains 96 columns. Both preserve the panel aspect. Fine clouds retain the original clean distance-field contours and approximately one texel of edge coverage, with 31-tone Bayer ramps. The established vertical lighting carries most of the appearance; a 16% contribution from smoothly merged ellipsoid normals adds restrained volume without noisy surface texture or random silhouette distortion. Fine presentation uses linear sampling for fractional display scaling; block presentation uses nearest sampling. Rendering targets 24 fps. Browser checks do not establish target-device frame rate.

The accent anchors are orange `#ff7a1a`, yellow `#ffdc1f` and azure `#159cff`. Header dots and weather meters use these same tokens. Small accents retain their colour purity; large sky fields use natural azure and cool blue-grey in daylight, orange/gold with restrained violet at sunset, and deep navy with blue-white light in storms. Rain, fog and wind highlights avoid fluorescent cyan. A 0.97 output ceiling, restrained highlight lift and reduced bloom control large light areas. The Canvas fallback uses the same grade and corresponding palettes.

Keep the header, hero, metrics and settings surfaces in the shared neutral paper colour. Weather-linked colour belongs in the cloud-cover meter: sunny scenes use yellow/blue, dawn pink/blue, sunset violet/orange/deep blue, night pale gold/slate, rain and storm blue-grey, snow ice blue, overcast cool grey and fog warm grey/sage. Each scene also gives the unfilled track a quiet related tint. Fill width still represents cloud percentage. Its palette is painted at full-track scale rather than compressed into the fill, with a soft trailing edge and a faint full-track wash. The percentage symbol uses the text ink and a larger proportional size. Colour is illustrative, not another measurement. Do not tint whole data cards to match the sky.

GPU motion runs on a continuous elapsed-time clock, with no short master loop. Clouds keep fixed natural silhouettes and drift in one direction at different speeds, wrapping only when completely offscreen. Their full crossings take minutes. Atmospheric light, star twinkles, sea shimmer and the three precipitation layers use independent rates. Lightning timing and horizontal position vary in successive 32-second scheduling windows; meteor timing and position vary in 53-second windows. These are event scheduling windows, not whole-scene loops. Pause/resume preserves elapsed time and reduced-motion freezes the illustration. The legacy Canvas fallback retains its simpler eight-second animation and does not implement the full GPU system.

Dawn and sunset use a level sea horizon. Wavelet spacing increases toward the viewer, and irregular horizontal fragments spread into a wider reflection path in the foreground. Warm highlights shimmer at staggered rates, with sparse brighter glints. The sea retains subdued blue-grey and navy tones rather than a bright cyan field. Sun shafts use a few broad, softly dithered atmospheric bands, covered by the foreground cloud layers, instead of narrow mechanical spokes.

Dawn uses a low sun to the left, cool rose/blue light, naturally rounded cloud banks with thin high cirrus and two distant birds. Sunset puts the sun to the right, with warmer thick clouds, copper reflections and a nearer flock of five. Bird passages vary in successive 83-second scheduling windows. Solar height changes slowly in the appropriate direction, and reflection position follows the solar position. Sun granulation, lunar surface marks and the crescent are illustrative artwork, not a solar/lunar observation feed.

Night has three star fields with different density, size, brightness and independent twinkle rates, including gold points and sparse warm glints, plus a faint broad sky band. The Moon and its halo share a slow local-transmission envelope while surface marks stay stable. Fog uses fine continuous density ramps, a distant ridge, a low island and a striped lighthouse with a tapered cylindrical tower, a shaded side and lantern gallery. A two-step foundation, contact/cast shadows and a small gravel lip anchor the tower in a low island, whose diffuse surface shading blends into wet rocks and a soft shoreline. Perspective wavelets and broken coastal ripples tie that shore to the water. Advecting veils obscure/reveal these landmarks and a warm beacon sweeps back and forth over roughly 42 seconds, changing its direction, spread and visibility through the mist. Wind uses a smaller pale-gold sun filtered by thin haze, distinct from the clear scene’s orange sun. It retains the established flattened cloud form with small proportion variations, and uses three trail layers with different widths, opacity, speed and length. The clear-sky composition stays fixed while its sun uses a red-orange/gold surface ramp and the sky retains blue, peach and restrained violet variation. Dawn additionally has thin high cloud and a faint near-horizon sea mist.

Cloud coverage modulates the common atmospheric-light envelope, while the light origin drives the restrained cloud-volume contribution. Rain origins follow the complete lower envelope of all moving cloud lobes and account for lateral drift. The displaced source must also lie within the current horizontal footprint, and cloud coverage clips precipitation from cloud faces. These are deliberate stylized approximations. The natural-behavior references and their implementation boundaries are documented in [natural phenomena](NATURAL-PHENOMENA.md).

## Independent instrument page

The second page intentionally uses a separate, scoped design system: warm ink, cream, coral, ochre and pale cyan nested surfaces, a thin lavender outer field, Space Grotesk/Rajdhani/IBM Plex Mono with a renamed Noto Sans SC Chinese subset, mixed module shapes, a one-third-width transect and compact nested data groups. Metric titles use a shared 20–29px role rather than miniature captions; numeric readings use tabular figures, disabled kerning and nonnegative tracking. A single owner displays each metric, with its related trend in the same component. Its twenty-eight modules and lower-right purpose dial are described in [instrument panel](INSTRUMENT-PANEL.md). Weather-page typography, icons and neutral data surfaces retain their existing rules. Coupling is expressed through information selection, never physical gears or chains.

Current observation composition uses four independent data lanes and 28 modules. The dial and primary each occupy 2/9 width; the single context strip sits below the primary. Scalar strips have compact fixed bases while actual charts absorb available space. No priority sidebars remain. Restrained coral/ochre heading ink sits on neutral groups; the primary alone has a muted coral surface. Humidity arcs use dark ink or deep rust appropriate to their background, the Moon is 128px in wide layouts, and visibility stays scalar.

The panorama is code-drawn WebGL pixel artwork with distinct frontal architectural silhouettes. Both pages use `resolvePixelGrid`; observation explicitly requests a 192-column basic grid, while the weather page keeps its 96-column default. Fine mode uses native density up to 1536 columns. Basic mode simplifies small framing and widens subpixel equipment lines. The two modes retain their own cloud silhouette/tone treatment. No generated image or image sampler is used.
